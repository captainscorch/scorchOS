# A tweet, a weekend, and a seat at the table.

On August 30, 2026, DHH posted that the Omarchy homepage had outgrown itself and that he wanted to spin up an Omarchy Design team. The application was simple: send a redesign to design@omarchy.org, and if it vibes, you're in. I liked what DHH was doing with Omarchy, an operating system built for the age of agents, and as a terminal and tech geek I wanted to be part of that. I had also just spent months on canvas-driven interfaces for my own projects. So I spent the day on it.

## The Pitch: omarchy.org as an Omarchy Desktop

My submission rebuilt the whole site as the desktop it advertises. A Waybar-style top bar with workspaces as section anchors and a live clock, the content tiled like Hyprland windows, each titled with the shell command that would open it. The wordmark rendered on a canvas, decrypting on load and scrambling under the cursor. And the part that landed: a theme switcher on the `t` key cycling through the real Omarchy themes, every palette taken 1:1 from the distribution's own `colors.toml` files.

- **No build, no dependencies:** One static HTML file, JetBrains Mono as the only typeface. It felt right for a project whose creator has strong opinions about build steps
- **The whole site, not a mockup:** Manual with all 51 chapters, every news article, teams, patrons, sponsorships, meetups, a command palette. Live at [omarchy.captainscor.ch](https://omarchy.captainscor.ch) the same evening, [posted](https://x.com/captainscorch/status/2094139135971794958) under his tweet
- **The answer, the same night:** DHH replied by DM. "That theme switching looks sick!! … Want to join us in Omarchy Design?"

## Then the Actual Work

The team formed within days. Barış Girişmen's design became the foundation for the new site, and for one week in early September decisions took minutes in the group chat and merges took hours. I took on the sections that were still missing, refined ones that already existed, and shipped everything as pull requests against Barış's branch, one topic per PR, in the style the repository already had.

- **The logo:** The Omarchy mark in the navigation draws itself on hover, every stroke in its own order and timing. When DHH declared the new brand gradient a core identity, the mark got its five bands the same day
- **Momentum by the numbers:** A figures section with the foundation pledges as a step chart, ISO downloads with their yesterday, week and month, and GitHub stars with a commit sparkline, next to the news
- **The installer:** An install section that walks through what the ISO actually does, with the Try apps for Mac and Windows beside it and a note on what to do before booting
- **Teams:** All teams as avatar clusters on one line of the home page, and a teams page laid out the same way
- **People love Omarchy:** The testimonials section, quoting what people posted on X after installing it, with a link to more of them
- **The details:** The install command kept to two lines on a phone, the installer's note to one line, the mark's stroke order fixed after a refactor shifted it

## The Launch

On September 7, the redesign went live as the official omarchy.org website. DHH's [launch post](https://omarchy.org/news/2026/09/omarchy-org-redesign-launches-with-29-languages/) mentions me for the logo, news, install, team, foundation and testimonial sections. His tweet about the site, and [Barış's](https://x.com/BarisGirismen/status/2096912834382770503), say what it felt like.

## What It Taught Me

Working in an open source team with a founder who moves fast is a different discipline than client work. Every change is public, and the review is the merge. Taste questions get decided in the chat, in minutes, by whoever cares most. The site is never done, and that's the point.

Computers should be fun. Websites too.
