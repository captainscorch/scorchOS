# The Farm as a Pen Drawing

Hof Bissee is a nature hideaway in Bissee, a village of 178 people in the Holsteinische Schweiz, 50 minutes north of Hamburg. 17 rooms and cabins in three guest houses, Farmhouse, Corn Barn and Cottage, a restaurant, two spas, a private cinema, barbecue spots and event locations for 8 to 800 guests. The farm had the photos, the place and a seal with a lot of character. My job was a website that shows all of it and gets guests to a booking without detours.

## A Seal, a Nib, an Engraving

A first version looked like any hotel site: eyebrow, headline, card grid, the brand only in small icons. So I gave the site one idea: the farm as a pen drawing. The seal became the stage of the home page. It draws itself in, and its four fields lead to the restaurant, the rooms, celebrations and the spa. Engravings sit as paper plaques over the photo edges, every house with its own motif. The guest houses stand under arches like stable windows, sections end in drawn horizons with engraved hatching, and a wave line opens from the middle under every headline. The farm map is a pencil drawing with seven places to click. The footer carries a pencil sketch of the buildings, with birds over it and the newsletter as a postcard with the seal as its stamp. 32 engravings and 44 interface icons share one nib: I redrew the icon geometry in the stroke of the seal, down to the burger, the close cross and the social glyphs. The HB monogram draws itself while the page loads. The site stays light, even when the phone is set to dark.

## From the Photo to the Booking

The bookings run through RoomRaccoon, which can take room and dates directly. The website hands both over: pick a room, pick a stay, and the booking engine opens with exactly that. A stay below the minimum offers to extend itself. The room list filters by house, guests, dogs, breakfast and amenities. The filters live in the URL and render on the server, so a filtered list like "Cottage, with dog" can be shared as a link. A comparison puts the three houses side by side: breakfast, kitchen, dogs, hot tub, check-in. On the phone, the booking bar floats as a glass pill in thumb reach, built so Safari 26 tints its bars correctly.

## Restaurant, Calendar, Celebrations

The restaurant shows its hours live, with "open now" and the kitchen's closing time. The calendar has a list and a month view with categories, every date has its own page, "add to calendar" as an ICS file and a feed to subscribe to. The weekly dates, brunch, spritz hour, yoga, run through it as a ticker. Celebrations show seven locations, from a barbecue spot for twelve to a concert barn for 800 standing. The event calculator by EVENTMACHINE loads only after a click, so nothing from its servers reaches a visitor who does not use it. Vouchers, jobs with an application form, a FAQ and the arrival page with the hand-drawn map round it off.

## A Backoffice Instead of a CMS

Instead of a page builder, the farm gets a backoffice of its own: rooms and houses with texts, amenities, booking ID and a gallery sorted by drag and drop, a media library that writes AVIF and WebP in several sizes and requires alt text on upload, redirects with a 404 log, and the applications as a board by status, with a role for HR and deletion six months after the decision. Statistics count room views, booking starts, calls and routes per day, without cookies and without IP, so they work without consent.

## Fast and Findable

Every public page renders on the server and carries structured data: Hotel, 17 hotel rooms, Restaurant with opening hours, Event, JobPosting, FAQ. Every page has a Markdown twin for AI crawlers, and every photo gets its own 1200 × 630 share image cut around its focal point. Measured with Lighthouse on mobile against the old site, the room overview went from 33 to 4.5 megabytes, the Speed Index from 33 to 3.5 seconds, and nothing shifts while the page loads.
